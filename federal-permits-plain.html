The Federal Permits

A DOE categorical exclusion from full environmental review, a military airlift, and a new NRC rule that lets commercial licenses reuse DOE and military reviews. Here is what each federal record says, and what it says about the public.

Posted Oct. 29, 2026 · Updated Oct. 29, 2026
Read the full version with every source →

The short version

  • A new NRC rule takes effect Nov. 25, 2026. It lets a company seeking a commercial reactor license reuse reviews the Department of Energy or Department of War already did, "to the extent that those reviews cover the same scope."
  • Since Feb. 2, 2026, DOE can approve advanced test reactors without a full environmental review. It used that exclusion for Valar's reactor in Emery County. The form has no public notice or comment language.
  • Valar told the NRC in April it had no plans to apply for a license, and asked that the plan be withheld from the public. In August it filed a revised plan "outlining plans to seek NRC licensing."
  • An earlier Hive check of the 136 comments on the NRC rule found none from the State of Utah. Utah is a plaintiff, with Valar, in a lawsuit against the NRC over small reactors.

In plain words

There are three federal agencies in this story:

  • The Department of Energy (DOE) can approve test reactors. In 2026 it adopted a rule (a "categorical exclusion") that lets it skip a full environmental review for them. It used it for a reactor in Emery County, Utah.
  • The Department of War (the military) flew that reactor into Utah on cargo planes, landing at Hill Air Force Base.
  • The Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) licenses commercial reactors. Starting Nov. 25, 2026, it can reuse DOE's or the military's safety reviews instead of repeating them.

The company, Valar Atomics, told the NRC in April it had no plans to apply for a license. In August it said it does plan to. It asked the NRC to keep both plans private.

136 people and groups commented on the NRC's new rule. We found none from the State of Utah.

Questions to ask, and to whom

  • Ask: Gov. Spencer Cox and the Office of Energy Development — Did the state comment on the NRC rule? The American Nuclear Society reported the states filed to dismiss Texas v. NRC; is Utah still a plaintiff?
  • Ask: U.S. Department of Energy, Idaho Operations — Will future categorical exclusions for Utah reactors be posted for public notice before they are signed?
  • Ask: Sen. John Curtis and Sen. Mike Lee — How would residents near a reactor be heard if mandatory hearings end for uncontested licenses?
  • Ask: Bureau of Land Management, Price Field Office — When will the public comment period on Valar's Project Beehive application open, and what is its serial number?
