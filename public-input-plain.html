Plain words · Public participation · NE 100.03

Where the Public Could Weigh In

From federal rules to county hearings, every step we found in Utah's nuclear buildout and what each record shows about public notice, comment and hearings.

Posted Oct. 29, 2026 · Updated Oct. 29, 2026
Read the full version with every source →

The short version

  • Several federal steps had little or no public input on the record: DOE's categorical exclusion took effect before comment, and the text we read of DOE's approval of the Emery County reactor has no public notice language.
  • The NRC changed its mandatory hearing rule without prior comment. Public speakers at future hearings get five minutes each.
  • State bills had normal committee hearings. Emery County held a public input hearing. Carbon County's planning commission took no public comment at Valar's presentation.
  • Utah's campus law requires "consent-based siting" with "willing communities."

In plain words

This page lists each approval in Utah's nuclear plans and whether the public got a chance to speak.

  • Federal: some steps had no public comment before they took effect. The NRC also shortened how people can speak at future hearings.
  • State: lawmakers held normal hearings on the bills. A state advisory board met once in private about property.
  • Counties: Emery County held a public input hearing. Carbon County did not take public comment when Valar presented.

A 2026 state law says nuclear sites should go to "willing communities." It does not say how willingness is measured.

Questions to ask, and to whom

  • Ask: Your county commission — Will the county hold a public hearing before any nuclear or data center approval, and will minutes be posted in a format residents can open?
  • Ask: Office of Energy Development — How will "consent-based siting" under S.B. 135 measure a community's consent?
