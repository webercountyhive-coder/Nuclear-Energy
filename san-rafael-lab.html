The San Rafael Energy Lab

A state-owned lab near Orangeville where Valar Atomics' DOE-authorized test reactor reached criticality in June 2026. The rents, the county land sale, the Inland Port link, and the state permit we could not find.

Posted Oct. 29, 2026 · Updated Oct. 29, 2026
Read the plain-words version →

The short version

  • H.B. 410 (2024) set up the state-run San Rafael Energy Lab, which news reports say the state bought from Emery County. A state board's minutes list Valar Atomics' rent at $29,000 a month and Natura Resources' at $71,000 a month.
  • County agendas and a news report show Emery County moving to sell Valar a parcel next to the lab. The Inland Port Authority had added that same parcel, 112.70 acres, to its Castle Country project area in January 2025.
  • State law exempts waste storage at this kind of site from Utah's high-level waste law only if the state radiation division permits and licenses the project. We found no such permit for Valar.
  • The county held a public input hearing on nuclear in June 2025, and its April 2026 agenda listed a hearing on the land sale. Its minutes are Word files we could not open.

Where it is

General location only, near Orangeville in Emery County. Not a parcel boundary.

The lab and its tenants

H.B. 410 (2024) created the San Rafael Energy Lab and an Energy Research Fund with $2 million one-time. Utah News Dispatch reported the plan as the state buying the lab from Emery County, with $1 million more for operations through 2025. The Office of Energy Development runs the lab fund. Dr. Jaron Wallace directs the lab.

The Utah Energy Research Board's Nov. 12, 2025 minutes list the tenants' monthly rents "per the finance overview": Valar Atomics, three-year lease, $29,000 a month; Natura Resources, leasing "both labs," $71,000 a month; and NuCube, with an agreement and grant applications. The same minutes record a $2.2 million appropriation for the state's advanced nuclear institute and a $6.5 million used-fuel recycling grant application with Curio. Board member Mason Baker asked whether the "Greenfield State" standard used for Valar would apply to any private party; the answer recorded is that "it's an ongoing discussion."

Checked on the rendered PDF, page 3: "Rentals: $29,000 - Valar $71,000 - Natura - Monthly payments for rental." Page 4: "The Institute received $2.2 Million in appropriations this year."

The state permit question

H.B. 78 (2026) exempts interim storage of waste from a DOE-authorized test reactor at a state-owned facility from Utah's high-level waste law, but only if the project "is permitted and licensed by the division" (Utah Code 19-3-321).

What we found: no permit or license for Valar from the Division of Waste Management and Radiation Control, in DEQ's website, DEQ's document site, the radiation board's July 9, 2026 packet, or its Sept. 10, 2026 agenda. Other board files did not open. In the text we read of DOE's Ward 250 approval, "Utah DEQ requirements" appear only for non-radioactive hazardous waste. The radiation board's June 11, 2026 minutes say that once the reactor went critical, "oversight of the radioactive materials would shift from the Division to the U.S. Department of Energy."

DOE's form says spent fuel goes to dry storage for shipment to a DOE-approved facility, and low-level waste is under 1 cubic meter a year. Whether a state permit exists that would meet 19-3-321 is an open question, not a finding.

The land sale to Valar

Emery County's agendas show it moving to sell parcel 04-0019-0030, next to the lab, to Valar. The steps: a staging-area letter and the start of the sale (Mar. 17, 2026), a public hearing (Apr. 7), a purchase agreement with the county's Local Building Authority plus annexation into Orangeville (May 19), and a due-diligence extension (July 7). A local news headline (ETV News, May 28) said the commission "OKs Land Sale to Valar Atomics."

The Inland Port link: the Utah Inland Port Authority added this same parcel to its Castle Country project area in Amendment #1 (Jan. 6, 2025), listing "San Rafael Energy Research Center: 04-0019-0030 ... 112.70 acres." In a project area, the port keeps 75% of the new property tax growth for 25 years. The May 19 county agenda included a resolution to remove the parcel. We found no Inland Port board vote removing it.

Who spoke at the June 17, 2025 hearing

According to Castle Country Radio: Commissioner Keven Jensen opened. Ron Dunn, a staffer for Rep. Mike Kennedy, spoke for. Kevin Butler called himself "pro-nuclear, and... pro-coal" and asked for more transparency. Lab director Jaron Wallace said the lab shares information unless it is under a nondisclosure agreement. Susan Sitterud and Barbra Jones, an atomic-testing cancer survivor, spoke against. Sheriff Tyson Huntington spoke on security.

Emery County posts its minutes as Word files, which we could not open. Those minutes are unread, not empty. Audio recordings are posted on the Utah Public Notice Website.

Emergency planning

DOE's approval has no separate emergency-planning section. Valar told the Castle Dale council (Mar. 12, 2026) it had coordinated with local emergency management and posted a job for a "Head of Utah Site, Emergency Management and Security." No county emergency plan was found.

Questions to ask, and to whom

  • Ask: Division of Waste Management and Radiation Control — Has the division permitted and licensed the Ward 250 project under Utah Code 19-3-321? If not, where will its waste be stored, and under whose rules?
  • Ask: Emery County Commission — What price and acreage did the county agree to with Valar, and has the sale closed?
  • Ask: Utah Inland Port Authority board — Has parcel 04-0019-0030 been removed from the Castle Country project area, and does the port collect tax growth from it?
  • Ask: Emery County emergency manager — Is there a written emergency plan for the lab, and where can residents read it?
