The short version

  • Utah's Nuclear Energy Consortium advises the state and the Legislature on nuclear development. The Office of Energy Development director, Emy Lesofski, chairs it and also chairs three other state energy boards.

What the consortium is

H.B. 249 (2025) created the Nuclear Energy Consortium "to advise the office and the Legislature" on nuclear energy development (Utah Code 79-6-1201 and 1202). The director of the Office of Energy Development chairs it. The law sets some seats (one senator, one representative, and the heads of state agencies or their designees) and lets the director appoint "additional members with expertise in nuclear energy development." It must meet at least quarterly and report each year by Nov. 30. (Statute wording from H.B. 249 as read in an earlier Hive check, Oct. 24–25, 2026.)

In 2026, S.B. 135 gave it a new job: help review state law for barriers to a federal nuclear campus, including "co-location of ... data centers." That report was due Oct. 31, 2026. We did not find it posted as of Oct. 29.

The other energy boards

  • Utah Energy Council (Lesofski, Sen. Ann Millner, Rep. Carl Albrecht, Nate Walkingshaw, Curtis Wells). Its Mar. 31, 2026 agenda listed S.B. 135 duties, the federal campus, a "Valar Atomics at San Rafael Lab Update," and a closed real-property session. No minutes found.
  • Utah Energy Research Board meets at the San Rafael lab. Its Nov. 12, 2025 minutes list Valar's and Natura's lab rents (see the lab page).
  • Statewide outreach: news reports and earlier Hive research tie the office's $1.8 million nuclear outreach campaign to Envoy Public Labs; the contract itself was not found in the records searched. Downwinders Inc., HEAL Utah and Utah Physicians for a Healthy Environment announced a Federal Trade Commission complaint about it on Aug. 17, 2026. An Envoy job posting seeks a $25-an-hour "Weber County Canvasser" for before-and-after surveys in a 30-day pilot campaign; the posting does not name the client.
