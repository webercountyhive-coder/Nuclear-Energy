Utah's Nuclear Bills, 2023–2026

Fifteen bills and resolutions that built Utah's nuclear framework: a regulator, a fund, a campus law, and two exemptions from the state's high-level waste law.

Posted Oct. 29, 2026 · Updated Oct. 29, 2026
Read the full version with every source →

The short version

  • From 2023 to 2026, lawmakers passed bills that created a Nuclear Energy Consortium, a state nuclear regulatory office, an energy fund, and a law for hosting a federal nuclear campus.
  • H.B. 78 (2026) added two exemptions to Utah's law that otherwise requires the governor, the county and the Legislature to approve placing high-level nuclear waste in Utah.
  • One fund ties four bills together: radioactive waste taxes flow into it, and the nuclear campus law lets it be spent on the campus.
  • S.B. 135 (2026) names "data centers" in a required review of state law. Its report was due Oct. 31, 2026. We did not find it posted as of Oct. 29.

What happened, in plain words

Over four years, Utah lawmakers passed a set of bills that work together:

  • 2024: the state bought an energy research lab in Emery County.
  • 2025: they created a Nuclear Energy Consortium (an advisory board), an energy council, and a state energy fund. They also taxed radioactive waste and sent that money into the fund.
  • 2026: they created a state office to regulate parts of the nuclear fuel cycle, passed a law for hosting a federal nuclear campus, and asked the federal government for more state control.

The waste-law exemptions

Utah law says high-level nuclear waste can't be placed in the state without approval from the governor, the county and the Legislature. In 2026, H.B. 78 made two exceptions:

  • Fuel-cycle work licensed by the state radiation division.
  • Short-term storage of waste from a federally authorized test reactor at a state-owned lab, if the state division permits it.

The second one matches the state lab in Emery County where a company runs a test reactor. The bill doesn't name it.

A report that's due now

S.B. 135 requires a report by Oct. 31, 2026 on changing state law to make room for a "nuclear innovation zone," including next to data centers. We had not found it posted as of Oct. 29.

Questions to ask, and to whom

  • Ask: Your state senator and representative — Will the S.B. 135 report on a "nuclear innovation zone" be posted for the public before any bill is drafted from it?
  • Ask: Public Utilities, Energy and Technology Interim Committee (Sen. Owens, chair) — Why were the words "including safety measures" removed from H.B. 78's annual report requirement?
  • Ask: Division of Waste Management and Radiation Control — Has any project been "permitted and licensed by the division" under Utah Code 19-3-321? If so, which one, and where is the permit posted?
  • Ask: Utah Energy Council — How much is in the Electrical Energy Development Investment Fund now, and how much came from radioactive waste taxes?
